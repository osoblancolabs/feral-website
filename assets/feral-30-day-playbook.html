The four weeks

Everything after this part is there to look up when you need it. This is the order you do it in. The amounts below are what one person can keep up on her own. Feral's managed accounts post more than this, but they have a whole team behind them.

What one day looks like

  • Film and post two videos. The same video goes on Instagram and TikTok, about three hours apart.
  • Put up one set of Stories, one to three of them. Three days a week you add your link.
  • That is the whole day. Instagram and TikTok count your views for you, so there is nothing to write down.

Six days at 30 to 60 minutes, plus one filming day of two to three hours that puts you a week ahead. If a day runs away from you, post something you filmed earlier and film nothing new.

Week 1 Set up your accounts

  1. Day 1. Sort your profile out. New photo, shorter bio, landing page built and put in the link space. Switch to a professional account if you have not already.
  2. Day 1. Start following 10 to 15 accounts a day from the follow list, and keep that going all week.
  3. Days 1 to 2. Pick five kinds of video you want to make. Watch 20 of the reference reels inside those five.
  4. Days 2 to 3. Film your first batch, 8 to 12 videos.
  5. Day 3. Start posting, two a day.
  6. Opening a brand new account instead? Do the warm-up in the next section before you post anything. It adds about four days to this week. If you already have an account, skip it.

End of week 1: profile sorted, landing page working, five kinds of video picked, and you are already posting.

Week 2 Get into a rhythm

  1. Two videos a day, about three hours apart. Fourteen across the week.
  2. Post the same video to both apps out of CapCut. Save it to your phone once, then upload that file to Instagram and to TikTok separately. Never save a video off one app and upload it to the other. It comes down with that app's watermark and hidden file info attached, and the second app shows it to far fewer people.
  3. Two carousels, spread out across the week. Never two in one day.
  4. One set of Stories a day. Three days of the week include your link.
  5. One filming day, 8 to 12 videos, so week 3 is already in the bank.

End of week 2: around 16 videos up and a week of content ready to go.

Week 3 Keep going, start looking

  1. Post exactly as much as last week. Change how much you post now and you will not be able to tell what worked.
  2. Ten minutes with the tracker, once this week. Open your insights and copy in the videos that have been up two weeks: what kind of video it was and how many views it got.
  3. Two Trial Reels on the kinds of video you are least sure about, once you are past 1,000 followers.
  4. Two TikTok Lives, 45 to 60 minutes each.
  5. One filming day.

End of week 3: enough numbers in front of you to start seeing what is working.

Week 4 Drop two, do more of the best two

  1. Ten minutes with the tracker again. Add this week's videos. It sorts your kinds of video from best to worst and marks the two weakest ones for you.
  2. Stop making those two. Put that time into your best two instead.
  3. Make your single best video again, three times. Same idea, different outfit, different place.
  4. Keep posting the same amount while you do it. What changes is what you film, not how much.

End of week 4: you know the five kinds of video to keep making, the two to drop, and what gets people to your link. That is your plan for next month.

Missing one day costs you far less than a stop-start month. Post something you filmed earlier and pick it back up tomorrow. Big gaps make it impossible to tell which kind of video was actually working.
Before you post

Setting up your profile

Most creators reading this already have an Instagram and a TikTok. If that is you, the card below is everything you need to do. The warm-up further down is only for opening a brand new account.

You already have an account

  • Change your profile photo to a clear shot of your face.
  • Cut your bio down. Most bios are about three times longer than they should be.
  • Put your landing page in the link space. Never your OnlyFans link.
  • Switch to a professional account and pick Public Figure as the category.
  • Link your Instagram and TikTok to each other.
  • If the account has been quiet for months, spend two days just watching and liking videos in your niche before you start posting twice a day. A dead account that suddenly posts a lot looks like a bot.
Check your account status twice a day. Go to Settings and activity, then Account status. Green means you are fine. Orange means something you posted got flagged and fewer people are being shown your videos.

Opening a brand new account only if you need one

Instagram and TikTok treat brand new accounts as bots until you show them otherwise. The warm-up is a few days of normal use that gets you past that before you post anything you care about. Do each app's warm-up separately. They check different things, so one routine for both passes on one app and fails on the other.

Instagram warm-up

Three days of normal use, then post. Instagram takes longer to trust a new account than TikTok does.

  1. Day 1. Sign up with a brand new email. Keep one Instagram account on the phone for the whole warm-up. Use a profile photo of your face, no cleavage. A photo that gets flagged hurts the account before you have posted a thing.
  2. Days 1 to 3. Simple bio, no link yet. Spend 20 to 30 minutes a day in Reels and Explore. Watch videos all the way through, keep likes under 30 a day, and leave five to ten real comments. Going faster than that looks like a robot.
  3. Days 1 to 3. Follow 10 to 15 accounts a day from the follow list, spread out through the day.
  4. Day 3. Switch to a professional account, category Public Figure, and add your landing page link. Both on day 3, not day 1.
  5. Day 4. Post three videos, then leave the account alone for 24 hours.

You are ready when your account status is green and all three videos got some views from people who do not follow you. Instagram shows you that split under each Reel. If all three show none, warm up for another two days and post three more.

TikTok warm-up

One long session, then post. TikTok gives you a number you can check after 24 hours.

  1. Day 1. Sign up with a brand new email. TikTok only allows three accounts on one phone. Profile photo of your face, no cleavage.
  2. Day 1. Put your Instagram name in the bio and link the two accounts together.
  3. Day 1. Spend 30 minutes or more scrolling the For You page in one sitting before you do anything else. Watch videos to the end, follow accounts like yours, leave comments.
  4. Days 1 to 2. Follow 10 to 15 accounts a day from the follow list.
  5. Day 2. Post three videos, then leave the account alone for 24 hours.

You are ready when those three videos add up to 400 views or more in that 24 hours. Under 400, warm up for another two days and post three more. Posting a lot through a bad warm-up just wastes the videos.

Profile

Your bio

Short bits, not sentences. Two or three lines and that is it.

What to put in

  • Keep it short. A few words per line. Never more than three lines.
  • Sound like you. Flirty, in your own words.
  • A couple of emojis, only ones in your colours.
  • Where you are from. A flag or one or two words.
  • One thing you want them to do. Short and flirty. Make them curious instead of telling them what you are selling.

What ruins it

  • Too long. Piles of emojis and random details make you look like an amateur.
  • One long block of text with no line breaks.
  • Lots of ways to reach you. It makes you look too available, and that makes you look less special.
  • A bare Linktree link with nothing telling them why to tap it.
  • A brand name nobody has heard of. Anything you promote has to make sense to a stranger.
Your page as a whole. Pick two or three colours and use them everywhere: backgrounds, clothes, makeup, hair, lighting. Shoot in different places on different days too. Five photos from the same shoot posted in a row makes your page look flat.

What your page actually looks like optional

Your colours only show if your page is photos. Videos land on it as whatever the first frame happened to be, usually badly cropped and nothing like the colours you picked. There are two ways to handle it. Pick one and stick to it, because doing half of one looks worse than either.

Keep videos off your main page

There is a setting that stops your reels appearing on your main grid. Turn it off and they live in the Reels tab instead. Your page stays photos and carousels. The videos still get shown to just as many people. They just stop deciding what your profile looks like when someone lands on it for the first time.

One setting, nothing to keep up with. This is the one we would pick.

Or pick a cover for every video

Keep the videos on your page and choose a cover image for each one that matches your colours. Done properly this looks better than the first option.

It does take time. One cover is a minute or two. At 25 to 40 videos a week that is an hour or more every week, on top of filming and editing. Work out where that hour is coming from before you commit to it.

Your link

Getting people to your page

The more choices you give people, the fewer of them tap anything. Give them one.

Build a landing page and put that link in your bio. Putting your actual OnlyFans link in your bio or in a Story is what gets accounts hidden or deleted. The landing page is the only link that ever shows on your profile, and you can set one up yourself in an afternoon.

Your landing page

  • One button. Every extra button means fewer people tap the one that matters.
  • Your colours and the same kind of places you shoot in.
  • Good photos. People judge this page just as hard as they judge your profile.
  • Say something only you would say. Not "more of me here".

Feral is happy with Linktree, Beacons, getallmylinks, or a page you build yourself. Any of them work. Pick the one you will actually keep up to date.

Your link in Stories

  • Point at it with an arrow or a button sticker.
  • Tell a tiny story first, then ask. "You are not going to believe what happened."
  • Give them a reason to go now: "only today", "be careful".
  • Three to five times a week, not on every Story. Doing it constantly makes people stop looking.
Name your link something that gives nothing away. "Link", "More", or your own first name. The word OnlyFans in your link name, on a button sticker or in your text is the most common reason accounts like yours get banned. Creators who do everything else right still lose the account in a week over this one thing.
  • Nothing in a caption, a Story or a bio says "OnlyFans", "18+" or "NSFW", or anything else that tells them there is sexual content behind it.
  • You only say where you are sending them on the landing page itself, after they tap, away from Instagram.
  • This goes for your highlight names and your button stickers too, not just your captions.

Filming

How to film it

Sort your light out before you worry about anything else. A badly lit video never looks good, however good the idea behind it was.

Camera

  • 1080p or 4K, 30fps. Either one is fine
  • Turn the grid on so you can line shots up
  • Wipe the lens
  • Turn HDR off
  • 4K fills your phone up fast, so move the files off it the same day

Light

  • One main light, straight at you or off to one side at 45 degrees
  • Point the light at you. Bouncing it off a wall or ceiling does not work
  • Nothing behind you brighter than what is in front of you
  • Turn every other light in the room off

Angles

  • Camera at eye level most of the time
  • Camera low down makes you look taller
  • Camera up high is better for your face
  • Film every setup a few times over

Sound

  • Mic 15 to 30 cm from your mouth
  • Use a trending sound, turned down underneath your voice

How long

  • 8 to 20 seconds for Reels and TikToks
  • Go longer only if the extra seconds actually add something

How to build it

  • Grab them in the first 3 seconds. Something to look at, hear, feel or wonder about
  • Get on with it in the middle, and hold back the good part
  • Give them the good part at the end
Pick one kind of light and never mix them. Either direct sunlight with nothing in between you and the sun, or a softbox with the curtains shut and every other light off. A basic softbox is 80 to 120W of real LED at 5500K, either 60x60cm or 60x90cm. Sunlight and a lamp together give you that murky look that makes videos look cheap.
Filming

Filming several at once

Four videos a day adds up to 112 a month. Each one takes five to ten minutes to film, so that is 20 to 40 minutes of your day. Filming a batch at a time is what makes that possible.

Every day

Film and post every day. Put aside time for four or more videos. Try to stay two days ahead.

Once or twice a week

Film on one to three days a week and post what you filmed. This is what most creators end up doing.

Once a month

One big filming day with proper gear. Only worth doing once you are already posting steadily.

Getting more videos out of one shoot

  • Change one thing at a time. Same idea, different outfit. Same outfit, different place. Four outfits in four places gives you sixteen different videos out of one afternoon.
  • Cut every clip more than one way in CapCut: the plain one, one with a different trending sound, one with words over the top. One clip turns into three or four videos.
  • Keep a spare album. Everything extra goes in one folder and gets posted in the order you filmed it, so a bad week never stops you posting.
  • Sort your phone into folders by kind of video, three to five of them, plus one for trends. Move the files across the same day you film.
  • Save at 1080p or 4K, then upload that same saved file to each app. Downloading a video out of one app and posting it to the other gets it shown to far fewer people.
Posting

Carousels on your main page

Five or six a week, three to six photos each, with a trending sound on them. Carousels are the fourth most common thing Feral makes and they are what holds your page together.

How to make one

  1. Take 30 or more photos in one go, one outfit, one lighting setup.
  2. Cut it down to 3 to 6. Posting all of them is the mistake nearly everyone makes.
  3. Put your best photo first, even if you took it last. The first photo decides whether anyone swipes at all.
  4. Order the rest so they look different from each other, not best to worst. Wide, then closer, then a close-up detail. Two similar photos next to each other and people stop swiping.
  5. Add a trending sound. Carousels with sound get shown to more people than silent ones.
  6. One set of colours per carousel. Mixing photos from two different days in one post ruins the set and your page.

Rules

  • One outfit per carousel.
  • No black or white borders. They look bad on your page.
  • Go easy on the editing. Heavy smoothing and airbrushing make photos look cheap.
  • No screenshots or adverts mixed in with photos.
  • If you put words on a photo, use the same font, size and position on every photo in the set.
Two a week is the least you should do, five or six is the goal. One 30-photo shoot gives you two or three carousels, so going from two to six is about picking better from the photos you already have, not another afternoon of filming.

Posting

Trial Reels

Post a Reel with Trial switched on and your followers never see it. It only goes to people who do not follow you, and it stays off your page.

How it works

  • You need a public account with 1,000 followers or more.
  • Switch Trial on before you share it. The Reel skips your followers and your page.
  • Instagram shows it to strangers through Reels and Explore instead.
  • It gets 72 hours. Instagram compares it to how videos like yours normally do.
  • Do well enough and Instagram offers to share it with your followers and put it on your page. Do badly and it just stays hidden.

How to use it

  • Two a week, once you have a couple of weeks of normal posts to compare against.
  • Only try things you have not already proved work. Trialling something you know does well wastes the go.
  • Use it for the risky idea, the new opening, the outfit you are not sure about.
  • If it flops, your followers never saw it. That is the whole point of it.
Posting

Stories and asking for the tap

Three or four Stories a week that ask people to tap your link, on your main account. Use all three kinds across the week rather than the same one every time.

The everyday one

Simple. A few emojis or a couple of words. Feels normal rather than like an advert. Keeps a trickle of people tapping and keeps your account looking alive.

The new stuff one

Words inside a sticker, or an emoji and a sticker and some words. "I have something new for you."

The sales one

An actual offer, said as quietly as you can. "My blue page." "Something special over here." "50 percent." Never say the name of the site.

Never put these in a Story: nudity, anything that looks like sex, rude gestures, sexual wording, dodgy links, very revealing photos with no context, or screenshots of adult sites. Every one of these makes Instagram trust you less, and you lose views long before you lose the account.
Fine to post: everyday life, selfies, talking to the camera, mild teasers, outfits that are sexy without being explicit, a clear line of text asking them to tap, and previews where you are dressed and posed normally.

Never do more than two of these in one day, and only on your main account. If you cannot post one on a given day, it does not go up. There is nobody to cover it for you, so plan around three days you can actually do rather than four you will miss.

Posting

Going live

Right at the start, 30 to 60 minutes of live a day brings people in faster than posting videos does. TikTok puts a live stream in front of people who have never seen you before.

Before

  1. Put the time in your calendar and keep it. A stream you do when you feel like it happens twice and then stops.
  2. Set your background up to suit what you are doing. Quiet music on.
  3. Post a TikTok right before you go live.
  4. Get your Instagram name on screen: pin it in the comments or use a green screen.
  5. Mark the stream as audience-controlled if what you are doing needs it.

While you are live

  1. Say people's names. Ask them to like and to chat.
  2. If you drop under 50 viewers for more than a minute, end it and start again. Never before you are five minutes in.
  3. Aim for 45 to 60 minutes in total.
  4. Know what you are going to do beforehand. A stream with no plan dies around ten minutes in.

After

  1. Delete the replay.
  2. Jot the numbers down on the Live tab of the tracker: most viewers at once, how long people stayed, new followers, taps on your link. Four streams in you will be able to see which kind of stream keeps people watching.

To go live on TikTok you need 1,000 followers, you have to be 18, and the account has to be in good standing. The follower number is different in some countries, and in Korea you have to be 19.

Streams that keep people watching

  • Cooking, gym, stretching or yoga, gossip night, getting ready, trying on clothes, gaming, ASMR, cleaning
  • Outfit wheel. Five tops and five bottoms on a spinning wheel, real or on screen.
  • Bucket of water. White shirt, with a follower goal attached to it.
  • Showing off a product, opening parcels, pets, a cooking challenge, makeup tutorials, having someone on with you, building something
Where you are is what keeps TikTok off your back. A bikini at a beach or a pool looks completely different to a bikini in a bedroom. The same dancing inside a workout looks different to that dancing on its own. Keep your lives clean and make it obvious what you are doing and where you are.
When things go wrong

If a post gets flagged

Check your account status twice a day. Green means you are fine. Orange means go and look at what got flagged.

A post got flagged

Delete it. While it is sitting there, fewer people are seeing everything else you post.

Your bio or photo got flagged

Change it. Swap a flagged photo for one with no cleavage in it.

Your link got flagged

Take the link off your profile and appeal. Do not put the same link back up.

"Removed content". This is the message you will see most often. It means Instagram deleted that one post. It does not mean your account is in trouble yet.
  • Only appeal if the post clearly broke no rule. If you appeal and lose, the strike sticks.
  • If it was borderline, let it go. Deleting it and moving on costs you less than trying to win the argument.
  • It costs you views, not your account. Your status stays orange and fewer people see your posts until it clears.
  • Do not post for 24 hours, then start again at two videos a day, not more.
  • Two posts removed in the same week? Stop making that kind of video, post safe stuff only, and wait until your status is green again.

If you are locked out or stuck on the login screen

Do this

  1. Turn off every VPN. Instagram locks accounts when it thinks you are logging in from somewhere strange.
  2. Stop trying to log in. Wait at least five days. Trying over and over in the first four or five days almost never works and can get you locked out for good.
  3. Try again from a phone you have logged in on before, in the Instagram app, not a web browser.
  4. If you are logged into another account: press and hold your profile picture, then Add Instagram Account, then Log Into Existing Account.
  5. Get the code by text message if you can. That is the one that works most often.
  6. If the first try fails, wait another two days before the next one.

People have got back in around 13 days after being locked out, with the VPN completely switched off.

Without these you probably will not get back in

  • Two-factor turned on through an authenticator app
  • A phone number that still works and can get texts
  • An email address you can still get into
Logging in from a computer or a web browser almost always fails while you are locked out. You get nothing back, or "Something is wrong". Stop trying it. Every failed go makes a permanent lock more likely.
When things go wrong

If your link gets blocked

Take the link down and appeal it. Then move people through a highlight instead, so your profile keeps sending people to your page while you wait.

First, deal with the block

  1. Take the link off your profile straight away. Leaving it up keeps the block going.
  2. Appeal it.
  3. Check whether the link itself is blocked, separately from your account, using Facebook's Sharing Debugger. Instagram and Facebook use the same rules about links.
  4. If the link itself is the problem, build a new landing page somewhere else and use that. Putting the blocked one back up just starts it all over again.
  5. If the appeal goes nowhere, take it to the Meta Business Help Center.

Then move people through a highlight

Set this up now, while your link still works. Thrown together in the middle of a block, it goes up rushed and looks nothing like the rest of your account.

Four out of every five things Feral posts to Instagram do almost nothing, and the worst fifth may as well not exist. Your account will work the same way. The whole job this month is finding which fifth of your videos is doing the work, then making more of those.

What to do about it

  1. Put your videos in order by views once each one is two weeks old. The tracker does that for you.
  2. Look at your best fifth and write down what they have in common. What kind of video it was, how you opened it, how long it was, what you wore, where you were, what time you posted. That list is what you make next month.
  3. Stop making your two worst kinds of video once you have two weeks of numbers, and put that time into your best two. The tracker marks which two for you.
  4. Make your single best video again, three times, with the same opening and a different outfit and a different place.
  5. Watch shares, not likes. Nothing else separates your best posts from the rest as clearly as how many people sent them to a friend.
Wait until you have two full weeks of numbers before you decide anything. Any earlier and you will drop a kind of video that was about to take off. Feral gives its own tests four to six weeks for the same reason.
